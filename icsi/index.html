01About the source corpus

This dataset is derived from the ICSI Meeting Corpus, recorded at the International Computer Science Institute in Berkeley and published by the Linguistic Data Consortium as ICSI Meeting Transcripts (LDC2004T04). The original release provides word-level transcripts for roughly 72 hours of English meeting recordings — about 795,000 words across 75 natural, unscripted staff meetings.

Recordings

75 meetings, 17–103 minutes each (~1 hour typical), captured with both close-talking and far-field microphones. Transcripts are based primarily on the close-talking channels.

Participants

53 unique speakers across the full corpus, averaging six per meeting (range 3–10), including many non-native English speakers of varying fluency.

Transcription

~795,000 words, ~13,000 unique word forms, in an XML-based meeting-transcript format synchronized to the audio.

Annotations used here

The summaries come from the corpus’s abstractive summarization annotation layer; attendees and demographics from its speaker metadata.

02What’s in this subsample

Each CSV row is one meeting with three fields. Every field is Base85 (RFC 1924 / b85) encoded UTF-8, so the raw text — which contains newlines, commas and quotes — stays on a single CSV line and survives round-tripping unchanged.

attendees_b85
Speakers who talk in the meeting, ordered by first utterance, with gender, age, education and language background (e.g. me018 gender=Male age=37 education=PhD lang=English/American/Calif).
meeting_text_b85
The full transcript, reconstructed from the stand-off word/segment annotation and rendered as speaker: utterance lines in time order.
meeting_summary_b85
The human abstractive summary, kept in its four labelled sections: [ABSTRACT] [DECISIONS] [PROBLEMS] [PROGRESS].

Packaging

The download contains one combined icsi_meetings_b85.csv (61 rows) plus 30 batch files of two rows each — batch_01.csv through batch_30.csv (the last holds the leftover row).

Decoding

In Python: base64.b85decode(cell).decode("utf-8"). Every field is validated to round-trip cleanly back to UTF-8 text.
